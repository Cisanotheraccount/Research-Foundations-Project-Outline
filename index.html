Rent as Terrain

Making Manhattan’s housing pressure tangible.

A research proposal translating asking-rent data into a digital terrain, a vertical drawing, and two possible material forms.

Situated technology

The map is not neutral.

Manhattan offers a recognizable geography of extreme rent variation, but the dataset and the hexagonal grid are both partial views.

Scope
Manhattan concentrates extreme rent differences within a narrow and recognizable geography.
Measure
ZORI estimates asking rents. It does not represent every signed lease or every household’s burden.
Coverage
Platform data reflects listed units and can underrepresent housing that never appears online.
Abstraction
Equal hexagonal cells support comparison while simplifying lived neighborhood boundaries.

Visual representation + rhetorical argument

Rent is not an isolated number.

It accumulates unevenly as spatial pressure.

Color shows intensity. Height makes pressure bodily. Line carries time. Warm-white material receives movement and light.

Material explorations

One research question. Two material strategies.

Primary Material Direction A

64-cell kinetic rent terrain

Sixty-four 3D-printed hexagonal columns form a physical Manhattan. The full digital simulation moves first; an eight-column mechanism tests whether the physical system can scale.

Prototype tests

  • Friction
  • Synchronization
  • Repeatability
  • Fabrication tolerances
  • Motor load
Alternative Material Strategy B

Projection-mapped Manhattan

A simplified white 3D-printed Manhattan receives the same rent timeline as projected color, labels, and motion. A calibration mode aligns the image to the model.

System tests

  • Model simplification
  • Projection alignment
  • Ambient light
  • Color readability
  • Playback output

Plan A is the primary direction, but its feasibility depends on whether a 64-column mechanism can move reliably at this scale. Plan B preserves the same question and dataset if the mechanical system is not yet dependable.
